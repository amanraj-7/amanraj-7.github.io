**Dissertation Chapter 4** · Arizona State University · Advisor: [Dr. Dwarakanath Ravikumar](https://search.asu.edu/profile/1869256)

#### The problem

US PV manufacturers are replacing aluminum (Al) frames with steel as a design-for-circularity strategy, because virgin steel has an 86% lower climate footprint than virgin Al and reduces reliance on carbon-intensive Al frames imported from China. This rationale rests solely on a comparison of virgin metals, even though secondary sources supply about 70% of US steel and 84% of US Al. It also neglects regional differences in smelter grid mixes, smelting technologies (such as basic oxygen versus electric arc furnaces for steel), and the added transportation burden of heavier steel frames.

#### What we are doing

We developed a geospatial supply-chain LCA covering 1,008 Al and 528 steel pathways across 126 Al and 88 steel smelters nationwide. It captures three pieces of information:

- The share of secondary metal in the US Al and steel supply
- The location, grid mix, and technology of each secondary smelter
- The shipping distance from each smelter to the module manufacturer

#### Preliminary findings

Contrary to the prevailing industry shift, a CdTe module framed with secondary Al has a lower climate footprint than one framed with secondary steel when the shipping distance between smelter and manufacturer exceeds 157 miles. This information can guide manufacturers' choice of frame material and smelter, and their ability to meet the Global Electronics Council's ultra-low-carbon module criteria.
