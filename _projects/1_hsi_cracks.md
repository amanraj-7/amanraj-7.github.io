**Dissertation Chapter 1** · Arizona State University · Advisor: [Dr. Dwarakanath Ravikumar](https://search.asu.edu/profile/1869256)

#### The problem

When a photovoltaic (PV) module is decommissioned, its physical condition determines whether it should be reused, refurbished, or recycled. Cracks are especially consequential: modules with front-glass cracks compromise electrical safety and must be recycled, whereas modules with limited cell cracking may be refurbished. Glass cracks are currently found by manual visual inspection, which is slow, subjective, and error-prone. Cell cracks require electroluminescence, photoluminescence, or infrared thermography, which are labor-intensive, need controlled illumination or electrical bias, and cannot detect glass cracks.

#### What we did

We developed a single framework that combines hyperspectral imaging (HSI) with a sequential machine learning pipeline. Applied to field-aged modules under direct sunlight from a 15-meter standoff, the method detected and distinguished glass and cell cracks without contact, electrical bias, active excitation, controlled lighting, or laboratory preparation.

#### Why it matters

Crack-type information lets PV asset managers decide at the de-installation site whether to reuse, refurbish, or recycle each module. We also measure the economic value of this information: without it, a reusable module may be sent to recycling, incurring a fee of about $15–45 per module and forgoing its resale value, while a glass-cracked module may be resold, exposing the seller to returns and liability.
