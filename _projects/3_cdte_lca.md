#### The problem

Cadmium telluride (CdTe) modules hold 34% of the US utility-scale PV market. Existing life cycle assessments (LCAs) of CdTe recycling rely on European inventory data from 2010, which cover only the older, frameless Series 4 (S4) design and exclude the secondary processing of recovered materials. Since then, module designs have changed significantly, and US conditions differ from Europe's in grid mix, transportation distances, landfill operations, and secondary material processing.

#### What we did

We conducted the first US-specific, industry-sourced LCA of CdTe PV recycling, using primary 2024 data from a commercial recycler in Arizona. We assessed the Series 4, Series 6 (S6), and Series 7 (S7) module designs across 10 impact categories.

#### Key findings

- Recycling yields net climate benefits of 3, 34, and 11 kg CO₂-eq per S4, S6, and S7 module, respectively
- Module design, rather than the recycling process, is the key driver of net environmental benefit
- Cleaner electricity and transportation fuels are the most effective levers to increase that benefit

These findings show that the environmental value of recycling depends on decisions made at the design stage, and they give US recyclers and policymakers a measured basis for prioritizing cleaner energy inputs.
