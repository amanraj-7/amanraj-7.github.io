#### The problem

An estimated 120 billion single-use beverage cups are disposed of annually in the US, and the quick-service restaurant (QSR) industry and governments are promoting a transition to reusable cups. Yet life cycle assessments (LCAs) of single-use and reusable cups disagree about whether reusables deliver an environmental benefit. Most exclude the upstream and downstream operations of manufacturers, distributors, washing centers, and landfills; overlook customer behavior and the geographic spread of restaurants and supply chains; and rely on dated inventory data.

#### What we did

We conducted an anticipatory LCA using primary industry data covering 6 cup-manufacturing sites, 19 distribution sites, 7,688 QSRs, and 1,229 landfills across 761 counties and 1,013 electricity grid mixes. Industry-provided data on customer behavior were used to model three outcomes for reusable cups: return to the restaurant, take-home washing, and leakage (cups lost from reuse). A systematic review of 355 prior studies confirmed the knowledge gaps.

#### Key findings

- Under baseline conditions, reusable cups achieve environmental payback in more than 50% of QSRs in 6 of 10 impact categories
- Cup manufacturing and the leakage rate are the most significant drivers of payback
- The distance from restaurants to the washing center and the grid intensity of washing electricity have limited influence

Publishing inventory data openly and accounting robustly for customer behavior are priorities for increasing the environmental benefit of reusable cups.
